## What Nerdy wants

Build and demonstrate an AI-powered product that genuinely helps someone learn.
Nerdy presents three prompts and permits an original idea. We selected the
provided **K-5 Math Game** direction because it has a clear learner, a concrete
learning domain, and a strong fit with the requested progression and mastery
signals.

The K-5 prompt asks for an interactive, gamified experience for elementary
students that makes foundational arithmetic intuitive and engaging. Nerdy calls
out innovative mechanics, steady progression, and mastery of core numeracy.

## Working interpretation of the K-5 prompt

This section records our practical reading of Nerdy's prompt. It is not an
official scoring rubric and does not lock the product concept.

- **AI-powered learning product:** AI used by a builder, such as Codex or Claude
  Code, is development assistance. The submitted product needs its own clear,
  learner-facing AI contribution; using an AI coding tool alone does not make a
  static quiz an AI-powered product.
- **Genuinely helps someone learn:** the experience should show a meaningful
  change in understanding or future performance, such as a mistake followed by
  targeted support, self-correction, and success on a transfer problem.
- **Interactive:** a learner's mathematical action should change the system's
  response. Watching content or selecting answers without meaningful adaptation
  is a weaker fit.
- **Gamified:** mathematical reasoning should be part of the repeated play
  mechanic. Points, badges, streaks, or animation alone do not turn a worksheet
  into a strong learning game.
- **Tailored for elementary students:** choose a specific learner rather than
  treating K-5 as one age group, then design the language, controls, feedback,
  challenge, and independence level for that learner.
- **Foundational and intuitive:** choose a concept that supports later learning
  and make its mathematical relationships visible and manipulable instead of
  relying only on a memorized procedure or trick.
- **Engaging:** encourage the learner to take the next meaningful mathematical
  action through clear goals, visible consequences, manageable challenge, and
  recoverable mistakes—not cosmetic rewards alone.
- **Innovative mechanics, steady progression, and mastery:** use a mechanic that
  exposes mathematical thinking, sequence challenges by prerequisite
  understanding, and unlock progress through demonstrated reasoning and transfer
  rather than attempts, speed, or one possibly lucky correct answer.

The exact learner, misconception, representation, mechanic, mastery measure, and
AI responsibility remain research questions. They become committed scope only
through `decision-log.md`.

## Required submission

- One working learning product or experience.
- An English description covering what it does, how it was built, and what would
  be needed next to make it operational.
- A functioning demo video no longer than three minutes.
- Third-party, open-source, and generative-AI disclosures.
- Name and email through the submission form.

Source code, a live deployment, and supporting files are optional but likely
help judges evaluate the entry.

## How evaluation actually works

- Human judges perform threshold review and final evaluation.
- No fixed public scoring rubric is promised.
- Judges may decide using only the description, video, repository, and attached
  material; they are not required to run the product.
- Therefore the product must work, but the three-minute narrative must also make
  the learner problem, AI reasoning, learning improvement, and product judgment
  obvious without extra explanation.

## Prize and hiring reality

- First prize: USD 10,000. Second prize: USD 5,000.
- Finalists may present to Nerdy leadership.
- A strong entry may lead to role conversations, but participation is not a job
  application and does not guarantee an interview or offer.
- Prize selection and hiring are separate processes.
