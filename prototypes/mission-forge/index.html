Concept review (adult operator)

This page is a scripted concept prototype for product evaluation. The story interpreter is a small local script that recognises a few known wordings and selects one of two closed mission schemas; it is not AI and makes no network call. All counts, roles and pass/fail checks come from those deterministic schemas. It does not measure or claim any learning effect.

Questions this slice should answer

  1. Does the opening communicate the fantasy and first action within 10 seconds?
  2. Does story → playable world feel like a meaningful transformation?
  3. Is Robo's mistake understandable without an adult explanation?
  4. Does debugging require mathematical thinking rather than answer entry?
  5. Does the transfer mission expose what the quotient counts?
  6. Does the experience feel more like a game than a decorated worksheet?
  7. Play both examples. Does changing only the story — and therefore the labels, the world, Robo's mistake, the repair and the transfer — make the story step feel necessary rather than decorative?
  8. If real AI later replaced the interpreter, would its contribution be visible and necessary?

What is scripted here

  • Story input is real and editable, and it genuinely decides the mission: 3 in each ship builds 4 ships of 3, 3 ships share builds 3 ships of 4, and each one changes the labels, Robo's wrong world, the repair action and the transfer. Only a small family of wordings for those two meanings is recognised; anything else gets an honest refusal.
  • There are exactly two meanings. A real version would have to read many more, and decide between them from short, often ambiguous text.
  • Robo's mistake is scripted, not generated: he always builds the opposite of the meaning the script read.
  • "Teach Robo" uses fixed choice slots. A real version would read a free-form explanation and ask one grounded clarifying question.
